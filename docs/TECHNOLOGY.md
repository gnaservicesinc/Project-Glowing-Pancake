# Technology and research design

Design baseline 1.3 · 2026-11-07 · Reviewed proposal, not a runtime schema or shipped catalog

Technologies are things an institution can understand, implement and use. They
change feasible model configurations, data workflows, controls, services and
infrastructure. A discovery is neither a ready product nor a global bonus to
every activity. This plan integrates the supplied proposals with the current
Lab, accounts, capacity, market, governance, event and persistence systems.
The [candidate catalog](TECHNOLOGY_CATALOG.md) makes the branches concrete;
[campaign flow](CAMPAIGN_FLOW.md) defines the surrounding player experience.

The long PDF also combines unrelated methods into single rows and sometimes
creates circular requirements or treats commercial availability as scientific
discovery. Split a row only when its parts create distinct decisions. For
example, Transformer architectures do not alone produce a reliable consumer
chatbot; mixed precision is not a blanket accuracy penalty; post-quantum
cryptography is not reserved for the distant future. Historical dates and
scientific claims need primary sources, not confidence borrowed from the PDFs.

## Knowledge, implementation, permission and evidence

Keep four separate questions for every organization:

1. **Is the method known?** Public knowledge, an internal discovery or a
   privately received specification can make a topic available.
2. **Can we implement it?** Relevant staff, integration work, compatible tools,
   data and finite experiments create a usable variant.
3. **May we use/distribute it?** Contract scope, actual rights, charter and
   applicable rules determine permitted applications, regions and transfers.
4. **What has been demonstrated?** Internal estimates, reproduced findings,
   independent evaluations and field outcomes support different claims.

Suggested lifecycle: unavailable → available → attempting → discovered →
implemented → validated, with failed attempts, archived variants and expired
permissions represented separately. “Publicly known” is not “installed in every
company,” and “validated” names a particular evaluation scope/date rather than
universal validity. A license expiry can remove permission without deleting
knowledge or previously settled work; explain any grace and existing-product
terms before signing. Never erase a historical model's provenance.

Every starting feature maps to a baseline node. Public baseline knowledge is
available at EEY 0; the opening route supplies only its actual installed tools,
qualified people and permissions. Classical methods remain commercially useful.
The player can acquire an advanced service without owning its underlying IP,
just as leasing compute does not require inventing its chip fabrication process.

## Acquisition, disclosure and competition

| Route | What is acquired | What it costs or leaves unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Internal research | A dated result and selected variant, including reusable partial findings | Qualified staff-days, budgeted experiments, cash and uncertainty |
| Public adoption | Access to published method/specification and declared licenses | Zero method access fee; finite adaptation/reproduction/integration, compatible assets and independent evaluation |
| Commercial license | Specified implementation/use rights and any promised support/artifacts | Fee/royalty, term, territory/use, capacity, exclusivity, transfer/publication restrictions and renewal risk |
| Partnership/commission | Defined research or implementation deliverables | Real counterparty resources, milestones, attribution and ownership terms; failure/delay risk |
| Purchase/lease a capability | A functioning component or supplier service | Recurring price, availability, compatibility and supplier dependence; not automatic research ownership |

The latter two support the first three instead of introducing free discovery.
An external foundry or specialist vendor can satisfy an installed-capability
requirement without giving the buyer a chemistry research department. Distinguish
**knowledge prerequisites** from **operating requirements** in every node.

At discovery, offer keep secret, announce capabilities, publish specified
artifacts, or archive/delete the internal implementation. These are policies
with transitions, not four permanently exclusive global states. A secret variant
can later be licensed or published; an already public artifact cannot be made
secret by clicking a different option. Announcements require evidence for their
claims and may affect interest; they do not automatically increase demand,
valuation or every rival's research speed by 15%.

“Proprietary” is not a synonym for “patented.” A patent filing, if modeled,
discloses material and has its own scope, term and enforcement cost. A paper
does not automatically release code, data or weights, and access to code does
not imply rights to its training data. The reference Transformer node should
be called **Transformer sequence architecture**, crediting the original paper;
an alternate-history player discovery must not falsely claim authorship of the
real paper. Use fictional labs and appropriately credited technical concepts.

Archiving/destroying a result disables that organization's selected implementation
and can abandon an unfinished line. It retains spent costs, publication and
license obligations, learned history and outside copies. Dependent deployments
need an explicit retirement/replacement review. Denunciation is a separate public
statement backed by evidence; research can be resumed if permissions permit,
without farming discovery credit. Standards need actual adopters and maintenance.
Neither prestige nor denunciation creates enforceable worldwide law.

Rivals follow the same costs and rights. Their knowledge of public claims differs
from their knowledge of private methods. Staff movement can convey bounded
general expertise under existing people rules; there is no automatic stolen
technology grant, real espionage tutorial or extra espionage minigame.

## Prestige, publicity and funding

**Scientific prestige** reflects credible, useful contributions. **Buzz** is
attention and can rise or fall quickly. **Trust** varies by audience and by
observed reliability, candor and fulfilled commitments. Separate these fields;
none is a universal morality, ethics or company-value meter.

Discovery significance and evidence quality determine initial publication credit.
An implementing independent adopter can add credit once per meaningful
contribution, not once per download, subsidiary, version or repeated adoption.
Public papers, code, weights and standards can get different credited audiences.
Track novelty/lineage so repackaging another lab's work cannot claim its original
discovery prestige. Reproduction and careful negative findings have smaller,
distinct contributions. Retractions correct influence without deleting history.

A provisional rational marginal-credit rule, compatible with integer arithmetic:

```text
adopter_credit(n, t) = A / n * (f + (1-f) * T / (T+t))
n = 1-based count of eligible independent adopters
t = elapsed days since publication; T > 0; 0 < f < 1
```

Compute once on an actual adoption with checked wide intermediates and one
rounding. The lasting floor applies to time decay of marginal citation credit;
it is not daily prestige income. An institutional influence score saturates
within a published range; the historical citation count remains separate.
Keep adopter identities and paid/installed evidence sufficient to prevent
subsidiary or abandon/readopt loops. Do not let unlimited invented companies
mint unlimited current prestige. Small credit can round to zero; do not promise
a visible positive integer award forever.

Funding offers weigh evidence, team, mission fit, market opportunity, buzz and
prestige alongside runway, terms and the backer's own budget. Publication is
critical to some scientific and philanthropic routes; paying customers or an
independent demonstration can support a credible private-research route.
Publishing may help rivals after adoption work, and headlines can outrun
results. Neither publishing nor high prestige automatically deposits VC cash.

## Research projects and variants

A project starts only when prerequisites, rights, people, tools, spare capacity
and cash/commitments are feasible. Preview effort, known bottlenecks, plausible
outcomes, optional choices and retained costs if stopped. Progress uses the
existing finite staff-day/experiment-work ledger. A failure yields a concrete
finding and bounded reusable credit; no button-frequency rerolls.

Important nodes support:

- **Planning:** choose target task and a tradeoff, such as small-hardware serving
  versus a high-capacity implementation. Show feasible operating requirements.
- **During:** a real finding can offer broaden scope, accept a limitation,
  investigate, pause or stop. Use a saved once-only progress boundary where
  an authored mid-project choice is appropriate; not every node needs 50%.
- **Completion:** choose verification and integration scope, then a disclosure
  policy. Better evidence costs work and may expose a limitation. It cannot
  magically convert an efficiency gain into prestige or vice versa.

Standing policies can resolve routine choices within stated spending, safety,
rights and publication limits. Only consequential decisions pause. A research
director never automatically publishes secret work or accepts an unlimited
license. Late-company complexity expands the possible choices, not the number
of compulsory clicks per simulated month.

Variants record creator, selected choices, parent variant IDs, evidence, rights,
compatibility, method outputs and maintenance needs. A company may own or
license multiple variants, but a particular recipe chooses compatible slots.
The best parts of two incompatible implementations cannot simply be stacked;
a hybrid needs its own integration project and evaluation. Alternative branch
choices apply to a variant, not an irreversible ban on the organization ever
studying the other path. Reimplementing a public method is worthwhile for
specific needs, not automatically superior to adoption.

## Dependencies, inheritance and bounded improvements

Use typed AND/OR knowledge prerequisites, installed tool/service requirements,
qualified roles, permission requirements and evidence gates. Organize by
domain → subarea → branch with tags for modality, application, era and cost.
Taxonomy membership does not create an effect or imply an exhaustive prerequisite.
Every optional future family has a feasible alternative when it is disabled.

Evaluate the selected parent-variant graph as a DAG. Reject cycles, impossible
AND/OR expressions, missing references and incompatible slots. A preview names
the parents actually selected; an unrelated technology that happens to be owned
does not contribute. Freeze project lineage on start, with explicit reevaluation
if a permission or supplier condition changes.

For each effect, record its target metric, scope, source, stacking family,
direction, compatibility and inheritance policy:

| Policy | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Direct/static | One feature or local metric contribution, applied once to its declared target |
| Diminishing inheritance | An ancestor contribution may decay as `m / (1 + k*d)` over the selected compatible lineage, within a family cap |
| Explicit complement | A named combination unlocks an option or changes a local recipe cost; both inputs are checked |
| Repeatable refinement | Paid work approaches a practical cap/floor; cannot generate free resources or universal immunity |

Deduplicate by original source across diamond-shaped paths. If several valid
paths reach the same source, use the authored canonical rule (initially shortest
compatible distance, stable-ID tie), never one copy per path. Materialize the
small resulting contribution set when a project/variant changes, not by walking
all research history every day. Do not propagate raw “accuracy” across unrelated
modalities or multiply all ancestor modifiers exponentially.

For beneficial unbounded-unit metrics such as throughput, choose a practical
scenario limit `Smax` and a starting value `S0`. One illustrative repeat curve is
`S(n) = S0 + (Smax-S0)*n/(K+n)`, for `K>0`. For cost/error, approach a meaningful
floor with `C(n) = Cmin + (C0-Cmin)*K/(K+n)`. Each mathematical step improves but
gains diminish; finite stored/display precision may produce a plateau. Evaluate
from base and level, not by multiplying rounded prior levels. These are game
abstractions, not measured technological laws.

Staff, material, power, servicing and maintenance floors remain. Stronger
mitigation cannot reduce all hazards to zero; a risk modifier applies only to
its modeled exposure. Rates stay within validated ranges, denominators remain
positive and incompatible reductions never sum to negative costs. A new
breakthrough can raise a bound only with explicit scenario content, resource
needs and fresh validation. Repeat levels/work use checked integers; reaching
an arithmetic/content limit reports a safe ceiling rather than overflowing.

## Technology eras versus gameplay stages

EEY is an **era end target** measured as elapsed years in the standard century.
It describes an approximate frontier pacing range, not a date on which the
player receives a technology. A node also has an individual target offset,
uncertainty and scenario eligibility. The [catalog](TECHNOLOGY_CATALOG.md)
uses illustrative offsets within these bands.

| Era | EEY cap | Standard reference span | Content rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing baseline | 0 | Known by the 2015 opening | Public knowledge and scenario-installed starting capabilities |
| Documented research directions | 11 | 2015–2026 | Dated primary sources; distinguish first publication from deployment maturity |
| Near future | 25 | After the reference interval through roughly 2040 | Conditional improvements extrapolated from known work; no claim of inevitable delivery |
| Medium future | 50 | Roughly 2040–2065 | Explicit science-fiction branches with resources, uncertainty and plateau alternatives |
| Distant future | 95 | Roughly 2065–2110 | Optional scenario assumptions, bounded physical/institutional effects |
| Campaign culmination | 100 | Roughly 2110–2115 | Integration, legacy and objective consequences; no mandatory final invention |
| Repeatable refinement | No arrival deadline | After an eligible branch reaches its refinement gate, including post-game | Paid, diminishing improvement with practical bounds |

For a custom N-year game, use `offset_days = round(reference_offset * N / 100)`
under a documented calendar conversion to set pacing targets. Do not compress
supplier delivery times, human childhood or biological age automatically.
Custom campaign dates/longevity are disclosed settings; the canonical biography
stays 1997 → 2015 → 2115. Shorter games can adjust starting knowledge and project
budgets, with feasibility checks. A historical-calendar preset preserves dated
public releases; an accelerated alternate-history preset may rescale them and
labels that difference. One EEY value cannot serve both semantics silently.

World research and rival/public institutions supply credible progress, with
finite scenario budgets and the same implementation rules. They do not all
receive unlocked technologies on an era boundary. Future research can stall.
Boomtown requires an actual adopted service, Maturing requires observed market
and control developments, and a company can stay a specialist while the world
moves ahead. The player's increasing capacity meets harder evidence, broader
integration and physical/organizational bottlenecks rather than arbitrary
universal research inflation or forced participation in all ten domains.

Hardware breakthroughs unlock procurement families rather than retroactively
upgrading every owned rack. Training improvements affect new eligible projects;
deployment improvements require installation/rollout. Data rights and model
lineage persist. Public releases can help actual rival adoption, licensing posts
real transfers/royalties, and news reports committed evidence once. Industry
standards and audits affect eligibility only through enacted contract/policy rules.

## State, authoring and delivery

The proposed content record includes stable ID/revision, domain/subarea/branch,
grounding label and sources, reference offset, scenario conditions, typed
knowledge/operating prerequisites, staff/tool requirements, effort/cash/compute,
outputs, choice policies, variant compatibility, rights and disclosure options,
evidence/maintenance and any repeatable family. These are field requirements
for later schema work, not JSON that the current loader already accepts.

Company state retains availability, actual variant access, attempts/progress,
choice outcomes, source lineage, evidence, installations, publications,
licenses/royalty obligations, adopter receipts and refinement levels. Events
consume committed facts such as discovery, installation, publication and first
adoption; they cannot grant the result again. UI inspection shows **what this
unlocks, what is missing, why this parent was selected, what it costs and which
claims remain uncertain**. Graph exploration can be filtered by a player goal;
there is no requirement to display the whole catalog at once. Main-game layout
is deferred to the user's next discussion.

| Current owner | Reuse and gap |
| --- | --- |
| `src/core` Lab / Frontier | Finite research, data, model lineage, evaluations and queues exist; per-company technology variants and explicit knowledge/access/installation layers need extension |
| Accounts / corporate / capacity | Payments, obligations, ownership and real resources exist; recurring technology royalties, nonprofit restrictions and partner deliverables need explicit contracts |
| Competition / society / market | Public activity, adoption, customer work and regional outcomes exist; prestige/buzz, evidence-based standards and stage observation need integration |
| Runtime / content / Studio | One typed registry/compiler/flow pipeline exists; register the selected new facts/effects and forms, not another research interpreter |
| Persistence / session | Versioned state and recovery exist; add access/variant/project receipts and migrations with each selected feature |

Implement in cohesive batches after the opening and layout direction is settled:

1. Define the versioned default scenario/biography and legal-form/funding
   constraints, preserving legacy scenarios and saves.
2. Deliver research access, one meaningful variant tradeoff, public adoption,
   publication credit and one finite licensing agreement through a complete
   opening-to-first-product route. Reuse a small reviewed catalog subset.
3. Connect real service adoption/control evidence to stages; show a rival-led
   boom and a no-boom alternative. Add later company/industry transitions.
4. Expand the catalog, organizational/physical partnerships and future branches
   only when their mechanics and explanations exist. Finalize versioned content
   in the shared authoring path; candidate prose is not production content.
5. Add the full campaign review, boundary snapshot and post-game continuation,
   then balance strategy variety, repeatable refinement and long-run workload.
6. After gameplay/layout are final, create and offer the deferred first-play
   tutorial. Do not treat this design's explanatory text as that tutorial.

Focused acceptance must cover public access without free implementation, license
expiry without deleting history, paid royalties exactly once, two compatible and
two incompatible variants, diamond-source deduplication, a cyclic prerequisite
rejection, duplicate/retracted adoption credit, bounded costs/metrics after many
refinements, saved pending research choices, no product upgrade without work,
and viable specialist/nonprofit/plateau paths. Qualification of the complete
century and post-game range remains later balance/endurance work. No code,
content version or shipped-feature acceptance is completed by this document.
